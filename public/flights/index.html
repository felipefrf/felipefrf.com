Hidden-city fares

The cheapest ticket to your city is sometimes a ticket to somewhere else.

Airlines price a ticket by where it ends, not by where it stops. A ticket to a city beyond yours that connects through yours can cost less than the direct flight. You get off at the connection. This engine hunts for those tickets.

Pick a recorded search

Every fare on this page was recorded from Google Flights in August 2026 and has expired since. The searches are real; the replay re-runs the real engine over their answers, and nothing is typed in by hand.

How the engine works

  1. Price the direct flight

    One call to Google Flights (through SerpApi) for the route you asked, with the nearby airports in the same field: São Paulo is Guarulhos, Congonhas and Viracopos at once.

  2. Fan out to the cities beyond

    From OpenFlights route data it picks the busiest cities an airline flies to from your destination, then asks for a ticket from your origin to each of them, one call per city.

  3. Keep the tickets that stop at your city

    A ticket counts only if it is one through-fare that connects at your destination and costs less than flying directly to the airport where you would get off. Summing two tickets never finds this, so the engine never does it.

This page runs nothing live. Each search was made with the real app, which keeps every answer it pays for. Those answers were saved as recordings, and the real engine was re-run over them through a replay provider that refuses any question the recording cannot answer. The numbers here are its output, unedited.

recording.json→ReplayProvider→run_search()→detect_hidden_city()→data.js

Python, FastAPI and SQLite on the owner's machine, with server-sent events for progress. The provider sits behind a typed protocol. Over 500 offline tests, including one that rebuilds this page's data and fails if it differs from what is published.
